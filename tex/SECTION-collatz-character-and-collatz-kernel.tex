\section{Collatz Character and Collatz Kernel}~\label{sec:collatz-character-and-collatz-kernel}

Although the periodicity of reduce vectors constitutes a significant result, we cannot stop there. As we learned in the previous section, not all reduce vectors lead to positive integers; indeed, many reduce vectors eventually resolve into fractions as the depth of the backtracking increases. Consequently, we are more interested in determining the conditions under which a number can be traced back to an integer, as well as the distribution of the resulting values.

To deal with this, we have to introduce two opetators.
\begin{definition}~\label{def:collatz-character-and-collatz-kernel}
Given $b \in\widehat{\mathbb{N}}_\beta$, define
\[ \chr b = \min\{v\in\mathbb{N}: \alpha\mid(\beta^{v}b-\gamma) \text{ and } \beta\nmid(\beta^{v}b-\gamma)/\alpha \} \]
called the Collatz cahracter of $b$. If the set is empty, we say $b$ is a primitive source and define $\chr b = \infty$. We denote all primitive sources as $\mathbb{S}$, they will be discussed in \cref{sec:collatz-character-and-collatz-kernel}.
If $b$ is not primitive source, we define
\[ \ker b = \beta^{\chr b}b \]
as the source kernel of $b$.
Finally, given $B \subset \widehat{\mathbb{N}}_\beta$, we denote
\begin{align*}
    \chr B &= \{ \chr b \mid b\in B - \mathbb{S}\}, \\
    \ker B &= \{ \ker b \mid b\in B - \mathbb{S}\}.
\end{align*}
\end{definition}
\begin{proposition}~\label{prop:collatz-character-1}
    if $a\not\in \mathbb{S}$, we have $\chr a = \log_{\beta,\alpha}(a^{-1}\cdot \gamma)$.
\end{proposition}
And according properties of the discrete logarithm, we have
\begin{proposition}~\label{prop:chr-are-periodic}
    $\forall m \in \mathbb{Z}$, if $b + m\alpha > 0$, there is $\chr a = \chr (a+m\alpha)$.
\end{proposition}
According to this proposition, we have
\begin{definition}
    Define $\mathbb{S}_{i} = \{ b\in\widehat{\mathbb{N}}_{\beta} \mid \chr b = i \}$.
\end{definition}
\begin{proposition}
    If $i = \chr a$, then $\mathbb{S}_{i} = \{ b\in\widehat{\mathbb{N}}_{\beta} \mid b \equiv a \pmod{\alpha} \}$.
\end{proposition}
For primitive sources, this conclusion also holds, and we have $\mathbb{S}_{\infty} = \mathbb{S}$. Furthermore, from \cref{lem:modulo-alpha-of-f_1}, we have
\begin{proposition}
    If $\alpha \mid \gamma$, then $\Phi_1 = 1$, and then $\chr b = \left\{\begin{aligned}
        &1, &&\alpha\mid b, \\
        &\infty, &&\alpha\nmid b.
    \end{aligned}\right.$\newline
    And thus $\mathbb{S} = \{b \in \widehat{\mathbb{N}}_\beta: a\nmid b\}$.
    But if $\alpha \nmid \gamma$, then $\forall b\in\alpha\widehat{\mathbb{N}}_\beta$, $b \in \mathbb{S}$.
\end{proposition}
This proposition states that whether $\alpha \mid \gamma$ or $\alpha \nmid \gamma$ but $b \in \alpha\widehat{\mathbb{N}}_\beta$, these are all trivial cases. Thus when $\alpha \nmid \gamma$, given $b \in \mathbb{S}$. Or it is not reversible modulo $\alpha$, or
\[\Log_{\beta, \alpha}(b^{-1} \cdot \gamma) = \varnothing. \]
And $b$ is not reversible modulo $\alpha$ equivalents $\gcd(b, \alpha) > 1$. Hence, with these, we have
\begin{definition}
    Suppose $\alpha \nmid \gamma$. If $\gcd(b, \alpha) > 1$, we say they are normal primitive source.
    Others we call them abnormal primitive source.
    All normal primitive source are denoted as 
    \[\hat{\mathbb{S}} = \{b \in \widehat{\mathbb{N}}_{\beta} \mid \gcd(b, \alpha) > 1\} = \bigcup_{p\in\Pi(\mathbb{P}\mid\alpha)} p\widehat{\mathbb{N}}_{{\beta}}, \]
    all abnormal primitive source are denoted as
    \[ \tilde{\mathbb{S}} = \{b \in \widehat{\mathbb{N}}_{\beta} \mid \gcd(b, \alpha) = 1 \text{ and } \Log(b^{-1}\cdot\gamma) = \varnothing\}. \]
\end{definition}
Immidiately, we have
\begin{proposition}
    $\mathbb{S} = \tilde{\mathbb{S}} \cup \hat{\mathbb{S}}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proposition}~\label{prop:primitive-source-of-simple-collatz-system}
    If $\alpha\in\mathbb{P}$, $\alpha^{2}\nmid (\beta^{\Phi_1} - 1)$ and $\alpha\nmid\gamma$, then $\tilde{\mathbb{S}} = \varnothing$.
\end{proposition}

We call the Collatz system that arguments follow requirements in \cref{prop:primitive-source-of-simple-collatz-system} as primitive Collatz system or simple Collatz system, They have a lot of better propoties than other Collatz system. And they are precisely the type of Collatz systems we will be discussing in next section.
We can also verify that classic Collatz problem $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) = (3,2,1)$ is a simple Collatz system.

Now let's consider how to decompose the reduce vector. We may expected there's a minimal reduce vector at first, and then apply the \cref{cor:single-vary-rule} to adjust to approch our target. We may consider the Collatz characters definitely are the most suitable choice, such that
\[ \bm{a} = (\chr a_1, \chr a_2, \cdots, \chr a_n) + \text{ an adjust vector}. \]
However, not all character vectors are valid. For example, for $5$ in $(3,2,1)$ system. Since $f_{n}((1);5) = 3$ has no source value any more, we can't write the $2$-dim character vectors of $5$. To render it a strictly rigorous formula, we would have to introduce certain special elements that do not exist in reality to modify the definition of Collatz character of those primitive sources, or put our view into more big field like $\mathbb{Q}$. Nevertheless, we do not intend to delve deeply into this matter here. We provide another way to bypass this issue.
\begin{definition}~\label{def:chr-vector}
    Define
    \[ \wchr a = \left\{\begin{aligned}
        &\chr a, && (\ker a - \gamma)/\alpha \not\in \mathbb{S}, \\
        &\chr a + \Phi_1, && (\ker a - \gamma)/\alpha \in \mathbb{S}.
    \end{aligned}\right. \]
    Also we define $\wker a = \beta^{\wchr a}a$. Finally, we define
    \[ \wchr\bm{a} = (\chr a_1, \wchr a_2, \wchr a_3, \cdots, \wchr a_n), \]
    and then we can naturally derive the definitions of $\wchr\mathcal{A}$, $\wchr\mathfrak{A}$, and $\wchr\mathfrak{a}$. We call them are adjusted.
\end{definition}
Notice that the first component of $\wchr\bm{a}$ is not adjusted. Because we do not need trace it back any more.
With these, we have
\begin{proposition}
    $\Phi_1\mid (\bm{a} - \wchr\bm{a})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{definition}~\label{def:red-vector}
Define
\[ \simp\bm{a} = \frac{1}{\Phi_1}(\bm{a} - \wchr\bm{a}) \]
called the simplified reduce vector. If $\bm{a}$ is the reduce vector of number $a$, then the $i$-th component of it we also denote $\simp a_i$.
\end{definition}
\begin{proposition}
    $\bm{a} = \Phi_1\simp\bm{a} + \wchr\bm{a}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}~\label{prop:rewritten-collatz-rule}
    If $i > 1$, then $\beta^{\Phi_1\cdot\simp a_i}\cdot \wker a_{i} = \alpha \cdot a_{i-1} + \gamma$. And if $i = 1$, then $\beta^{\Phi_1\cdot\simp a_1}\cdot \ker a_{1} = \alpha \cdot a + \gamma$.
\end{proposition}

Let us examine the character vector $\wchr\bm{a}$ of $a$. We observe that it depends solely on $a$ itself; within a given Collatz system, it reflects the intrinsic characteristics of $a$. However, we must point out that, although $\Phi_1 \mid (\bm{a} - \wchr\bm{a})$ holds true, $\Phi_k$ does not always divide $\Phi_1 \cdot \simp a_i$. In other words, the growth of these $\simp a_i$ does not adhere to the pattern revealed by \cref{cor:alpha-times-between-Phis}. Since the "adjustment operation" alters the remainder of the source value (see \cref{cor:remainder-after-change-reduce-vector-modulo-alpha}), the adjusted character vector changes accordingly. In light of this, it is necessary for us to consider the impact that the adjustment operation exerts upon $\wchr\bm{a}$.

\begin{lemma}~\label{lem:modulo-alpha-of-f_1}
    Suppose $\simp a_1 = \chr a$, then
    \[ f_{n}(\bm{a} + k\Phi_1\bm{e}_1) \equiv\left\{\begin{aligned}
        &k\cdot\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}(\beta^{\Phi_1} - 1) + f_{n}(\bm{a}), &&\alpha \nmid \gamma,\\
        &\beta^{k}\left(f_{n}(\bm{a}) + \frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\right) - \frac{\gamma}{\alpha}, &&\alpha \mid \gamma.
   \end{aligned}\right. \pmod{\alpha} \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    If $\alpha \nmid \gamma$, trivial from \cref{cor:remainder-after-change-reduce-vector-modulo-alpha}.\newline
    If $\alpha \mid \gamma$, then from \cref{exm:sequence-of-Phi}, $\Phi_1 = 1$, thus we have
    \begin{align*}
        A_{k} =& \frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\beta^{k\Phi_1}\cdot\ker a - \gamma\right)  &&\ \\
        =& \frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot \beta^{k}\cdot\ker a - \frac{\gamma}{\alpha}   &&\ \\
        \equiv& \frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot \beta^{k}\cdot (\alpha A_0 + \gamma) - \frac{\gamma}{\alpha}   &&\pmod{\alpha}\\
        \equiv& \beta^{k}\left(A_0 + \frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\right) - \frac{\gamma}{\alpha}.   &&\pmod{\alpha}
    \end{align*}
\end{proof}
